PART I

The Creator: Ultimate Ground, Origin, and Cause

Every serious inquiry into existence traces back to one irreducible question: what is the ultimate ground of all that is? Using the idea of a limit in mathematics as an analogy, God may be understood as the one reality to which our deepest questions of where, when, and why ultimately converge: the foundational ground of reality, the primordial origin of time, and the ultimate explanation of causation. In this view, questions such as who created the creator or what came before the creator are category mistakes—they apply the logic of dependent things to that which is proposed as the ultimate ground of all dependent reality.

Many thinkers across traditions approach this question from different angles, yet their arguments converge within this framework. Aquinas contends that dependent things require a first cause [1], while Leibniz maintains that the universe must have a sufficient reason beyond itself [2, 3]. Swinburne proposes that God is the most reasonable explanation for the existence and order of the universe [4], and Gale and Pruss demonstrate that contingent reality cannot fully explain itself and must rest on a necessary ground [5]. From the East, Laozi describes the Tao as “something formless and perfect before the universe was born”—eternal, unchanging, the mother of all things [6]. Together, these perspectives point to a shared insight: the world is better understood not as something that simply exists by itself, but as a contingent reality that points beyond itself to God—like a sequence converging to its limit—the creator, the first cause, and the ultimate ground of all being.
