THE FOUNDATIONAL FRAMEWORK

A way of understanding
existence and purpose.

The framework begins with the Creator as the ultimate ground of existence, turns to humanity's nature and calling, and opens toward the practical question of how to live meaningfully in the modern world.

This is an evolving work. The first two parts reflect the current foundational manuscript; Part III is a developing area.

PART I

The Creator: Ultimate Ground, Origin, and Cause

Exploring the source of existence and the relationship between Creator and creation.

  1. 1.1

    Creator and Creation: A Relationship of Fulfillment

    The manuscript describes creation and Creator in a relationship of fulfillment, with creation finding meaning in relationship to its source.

  2. 1.2

    The Yearning for Oneness

    A reflection on creation's longing for its source and the movement toward restored oneness.

  3. 1.3

    Visible and Invisible: Two Expressions of Creation

    The visible, material world and the invisible dimensions of order, law, and intelligence are considered as complementary expressions of creation.

  4. 1.4

    The Creator Known Through Creation

    Although the Creator transcends creation, the manuscript considers how qualities such as intelligence, beauty, goodness, and order may be discerned through it.

PART II

Humanity: Heaven’s Breath in Earthly Clay

Considering the human being as a union of visible and invisible dimensions, with gifts, relationships, and a calling.

  1. 2.1

    The Gift of Likeness

    Humanity's search for meaning, values, moral truth, and freedom is connected to the Creator's likeness and character.

  2. 2.2

    Relationship and Oneness: Humanity’s Twin Yearnings

    The human longing for relationship and oneness is explored in relation to the Creator and to other people.

  3. 2.3

    Soul and Body: A Life on Mission

    The soul and body are presented as joined in earthly life, with the body serving as the means through which a life's calling is enacted.

  4. 2.4

    Free Will and Intelligence: Gifts and Responsibilities

    Freedom makes choice possible; intelligence enables learning and deliberation. Together they bring responsibility for one's actions.

  5. 2.5

    Demands and Rewards: Existence with Purpose

    Material needs and life's enjoyments are acknowledged, while being placed in relation to a purpose beyond survival and pleasure.

  6. 2.6

    Divine Ownership and Human Possession

    The manuscript distinguishes ultimate ownership belonging to the Creator from legitimate human possession and its responsibilities of stewardship.

  7. 2.7

    Hardship, Suffering, and Greater Understanding

    Hardship can prompt learning and the prevention of future harm; the text emphasizes removing avoidable suffering as well as learning from experience.

PART III

A Meaningful Life in the Modern World

An open space for the framework's continued development and practical application.

Developing section

The current manuscript names this part but has not yet developed its subsections. New material will be added as the framework grows.

About this version

This website presents an introductory map of the foundational manuscript, A Healthy Belief System Towards A Meaningful Life. The summaries are concise editorial introductions, not substitutes for the full arguments. The framework is a work in progress.
